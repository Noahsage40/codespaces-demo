# Stryker Sage — Learn It From the Ground Up

A self-paced, 6-week study plan for learning the Stryker Sage business: the company, the products, the clinical problems they solve, the buyers, and how to talk about it.

> **About the sources.** Every product fact below links to an official Stryker page (stryker.com, investors.stryker.com, homecare.stryker.com) or an SEC filing. Read the linked page as your primary source. Product names, reorder numbers and claims differ by country and change over time, so check the current Stryker page or IFU before you rely on any detail. Clinical outcome figures are Stryker's own marketing claims unless noted otherwise.

---

## How to use this plan

- **Time:** about 5–7 hours a week. Each week has *Read*, *Learn*, *Do* and *Check yourself* steps.
- **Notebook:** keep a running "product card" for each product family (name → problem it solves → how it works → key claim → who buys it).
- **Rule:** if a fact matters, find it on the Stryker page yourself. This guide is a map. The source is the territory.

### Learn: key facts
| Topic | Fact |
|---|---|
| What Sage is | Sage Products, LLC, a maker of single-use, disposable patient-care products that help prevent "never events" and hospital-acquired conditions |
| Headquarters | Cary, Illinois (about a 600,000 sq ft facility) |
| Deal | Stryker agreed to buy Sage from **Madison Dearborn Partners** for **$2.775 billion** in cash (agreement signed Jan 31, 2016) |
| Closed | **April 1, 2016**. Stryker bought all equity in Sage Products Holdings II, LLC, and Sage became a wholly owned subsidiary |
| Size at acquisition | About **$430M** in FY2015 sales, up about 13% year over year |
| Strategic fit | Stryker said Sage "fits well with our **Medical** division". Sage's disposables complement Stryker Medical's capital equipment (beds, stretchers, patient-handling equipment) |
| Where it sits today | Stryker reports two segments, **MedSurg & Neurotechnology** and **Orthopaedics**. Sage's patient-care products sit inside MedSurg & Neurotechnology |
| Scale of the parent (FY2025) | About $25.1B total net sales. MedSurg & Neurotechnology about $15.6B. Orthopaedics about $9.5B |

### Learn: the business model in one paragraph
Stryker Medical sells **capital** (beds, stretchers), which hospitals buy rarely. Sage sells **consumables**, which hospitals buy every day. Consumables bring recurring revenue, protocol "stickiness" (once a hospital standardizes on a cleansing or oral-care protocol, it reorders constantly) and group purchasing organization (GPO) contracts. One example is Sage's sole-source oral-care contract with HealthTrust: https://www.infectioncontroltoday.com/view/sage-products-awarded-sole-source-oral-care-contract-healthtrust

### Check yourself

## Week 2 — The clinical problems (learn the "why" before the "what")

Sage's whole portfolio is organized around **hospital-acquired conditions (HACs)**. These are preventable harms that cost hospitals money, and some of them affect Medicare reimbursement. Learn these terms cold:

| Acronym | Meaning | Sage product family that addresses it |
|---|---|---|
| **VAP** | Ventilator-associated pneumonia | Oral care for ventilated patients (Q•Care) |
| **HAP / NV-HAP** | Hospital-acquired / non-ventilator hospital-acquired pneumonia | Oral care for non-ventilated patients (Continue Care, Self Oral Care) |
| **SSI** | Surgical site infection | 2% CHG pre-op skin prep cloths |
| **CLABSI** | Central line-associated bloodstream infection | CHG cleansing in ICU (Sage 2% CHG cloths per facility protocol) |
| **CAUTI** | Catheter-associated urinary tract infection | External urine management (PrimoFit / PrimaFit), which is an alternative to indwelling catheters |
| **HAPI** | Hospital-acquired pressure injury | Prevalon heel protectors, TAP / AirTAP turn-and-position systems |
| **IAD** | Incontinence-associated dermatitis | Comfort Shield barrier cream cloths |
| **SPHM** | Safe patient handling & mobility (caregiver injury) | AirTAP, MATS air transfer systems |
| Basin contamination | Reusable bath basins as an infection reservoir | Comfort Bath / Essential Bath disposable washcloths |

### Read
- Stryker HAP fact sheet: https://www.stryker.com/content/dam/stryker/sage/resources/Uncover-and-quantify-hospital-acquired-pneumonia-HAP-fact-sheet.pdf
- Stryker article, skin integrity and infection prevention (2025): https://www.stryker.com/us/en/sage/news/2025/patient-skin-integrity-a-critical-factor-in-infection-prevention.html

### Do
- Make flashcards for each acronym: definition, why it matters to the hospital (cost, mortality, reimbursement), and which Sage product addresses it.

### Check yourself
1. What is the difference between VAP and NV-HAP, and why does NV-HAP matter on *every* unit?
2. Why does an external urine management device connect to CAUTI prevention?
3. Name two HACs that a single Sage product family addresses.

## Week 3 — Product family 1: Oral care (pneumonia prevention)

### Read
- Oral care tools: https://www.stryker.com/us/en/sage/products/sage-oral-hygiene-tools.html
- Systems for **ventilated** patients: https://www.stryker.com/us/en/sage/products/sage-oral-hygiene-systems-for-ventilated-patients.html
- Systems for **non-ventilated** patients: https://www.stryker.com/us/en/sage/products/sage-oral-hygiene-systems-for-non-ventilated-patients.html
- Oral care brochure: https://www.stryker.com/content/dam/stryker/sage/resources/Sage-Oral-Care-Brochure.pdf
- HAP sell sheet (AU): https://www.stryker.com/content/dam/stryker/sage/products/sage-oral-care-systems-for-mechanically-ventilated-patients/au/HAP%20Sell%20Sheet_2825.pdf

### Learn
- **Q•Care**: comprehensive 24-hour oral cleansing and suctioning systems for **ventilated** patients, designed to remove plaque, debris and oral secretions. They're built around **Corinz**, an all-in-one antiseptic rinse.
- **Continue Care**: kits for **non-ventilated** patients at risk for aspiration pneumonia.
- **Sage Self Oral Care**: a toothbrush with Corinz antiseptic cleansing, for patients who can brush on their own.
- **Toothette**: Sage's long-standing suction and non-suction swab brand.
- **Key claims on Stryker pages:** a 70% decrease in NV-HAP and $5.9M in cost avoidance (attributed to an *American Journal of Infection Control* study), and a 66.7% HAP reduction at an NHS stroke ward (AU sell sheet, single site).

### Check yourself
1. Which system goes to a ventilated ICU patient, and which goes to a med-surg patient?
2. What is Corinz?
3. What evidence would you cite to a nurse manager, and what caveats would you mention?

## Week 4 — Product family 2: Skin, bathing and infection prevention

### Read
- 2% CHG cloths brochure: https://www.stryker.com/content/dam/stryker/sage/products/chg-cloths/resources/2023%20Sage%202%20percent%20CHG%20cloths%20brochure.pdf
- CHG cloths and SSI evidence (2024): https://www.stryker.com/us/en/sage/news/2024/2-percent-chlorhexidine-gluconate-cloth-ssi-prevention.html
- Surgery prep (Home Care): https://homecare.stryker.com/surgery-prep
- Comfort Shield barrier cream cloths: https://www.stryker.com/us/en/sage/products/sage-barrier-cream-cloths.html
- Comfort Bath (Home Care store): https://homecare.stryker.com/store/c/33-Comfort-Bath.aspx
- Trial using Sage CHG cloths in spine surgery: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02490631

### Learn
- **Sage 2% CHG cloths**: premoistened with an FDA-approved antiseptic, rinse-free, each cloth equivalent to **500 mg CHG**, and they stay active on the skin up to **6 hours**. Stryker positions them as free of benzalkonium chloride (BZK). They're used the night before and the morning of surgery (SSI), and per facility protocol in the ICU. Reorder #9717 (6-cloth pack) and #9705 (2-cloth pack).
- **Comfort Bath / Essential Bath / Impreva Bath**: rinse-free, disposable bathing washcloths (Comfort Bath has aloe and vitamin E). They replace basin baths, which Stryker presents as an infection reservoir. These are **not** CHG products.
- **Comfort Shield barrier cream cloths**: one-step perineal cleansing, deodorizing and protection with **3% dimethicone**, for incontinence-associated dermatitis (IAD). Reorder #7502, #7503, #7905, #7924. Don't flush them.
- **Comfort Rinse-free Shampoo Cap**: waterless hair washing.

### Check yourself
1. A customer asks whether Comfort Bath is the same as CHG bathing. What's your answer?
2. When is a pre-op CHG cloth applied, and how long does it stay active?
3. What does Comfort Shield protect against, and what is its active ingredient?

## Week 5 — Product families 3 and 4: Pressure injury, patient handling, urine management

### Read: Pressure injury and safe patient handling (Prevalon line)
- Heel Protector II: https://stryker.com/us/en/sage/products/sage-heel-protector-ii.html
- Heel Protector III (EU): https://www.stryker.com/fi/en/sage/products/sage-heel-protector-iii/index-eu.html
- AirTAP XXL: https://www.stryker.com/us/en/sage/products/sage-airtap-xxl.html
- TAP LC lift-compatible: https://www.stryker.com/us/en/sage/products/sage-tap-lc.html
- Injury prevention catalog: https://www.stryker.com/content/dam/stryker/sage/resources/Sage-Product-Catalog-Injury-Prevention.pdf
- Multi-Position MATS brochure: https://www.stryker.com/content/dam/stryker/sage/products/multi-position-mats/resources/Sage-Multi-Position-MATS-brochure.pdf

### Learn
- **Heel protectors (II / III)**: the heel is the *second most common* site for pressure injury. The boots offload the heel, reduce the risk of plantar flexion contracture and are compatible with sequential compression devices (SCDs). Heel Protector III also helps prevent lateral rotation, which reduces the risk of peroneal nerve damage. They're for in-bed use only, because walking in them is a fall risk.
- **TAP (Turn and Position) 1.0 / 2.0 / XL / XXL / LC**: help caregivers turn and position patients while reducing the risk factors for skin injury. The LC version is lift-compatible.
- **AirTAP**: an *air-assisted* repositioning system that significantly reduces the force needed to move patients. This links pressure-injury prevention to caregiver safety (SPHM).
- **MATS (Mobile Air Transfer System)**: air-assisted lateral transfers. The Multi-Position MATS allows repositioning and transfer on the OR table.

### Read: External urine management
- PrimaFit (female anatomy): https://www.stryker.com/us/en/sage/products/sage-primafit.html
- PrimoFit brochure (male anatomy): https://www.stryker.com/content/dam/stryker/sage/products/primofit/resources/Sage-PrimoFit-Brochure.pdf
- PrimoFit redesign (2022): https://www.stryker.com/us/en/sage/news/2022/sage-primofit-redesign.html
- PrimoFit Flow (2026): https://www.stryker.com/us/en/sage/news/2026/primofit-flow-advances-urinary-management-performance.html
- PrimaFit updates (2021): https://www.stryker.com/us/en/sage/news/2021/primafit-device-updates.html

### Learn
- **PrimoFit (male)**: continuous low suction diverts urine through a chamber to a canister to keep skin dry. The adhesive base means no sizing charts. The 2022 redesign added **65% more adhesive area**. **PrimoFit+** (EU/UK naming) can stay in place up to 24 hours.
- **PrimoFit Flow (2026)**: adds a foam stabilizer to prevent tube twisting. Stryker claims **2.8× better urine diversion** than PrimoFit+ in internal testing.
- **PrimaFit (female)**: replace every 12–24 hours, or sooner if soiled. Urinary retention is a contraindication.
- **Why it matters:** these are non-invasive alternatives to indwelling catheters, which supports CAUTI-reduction programs and IAD prevention.

### Check yourself
1. Name the two problems AirTAP solves at once.
2. What is the difference between PrimoFit and PrimaFit?
3. Why shouldn't a patient walk in a heel protector?

## Week 6 — Putting it together: customers, selling and capstone

### Who buys and uses Sage products
- **Users:** bedside nurses, CNAs and patient-care techs, OR and pre-op staff
- **Influencers:** infection preventionists, wound/ostomy/continence (WOC) nurses, nurse educators, SPHM coordinators, respiratory therapists
- **Economic buyers:** nursing leadership (CNO), value analysis committees, supply chain, GPOs (e.g., HealthTrust)
- **Value story:** prevent a costly HAC → avoid extra cost and length of stay → standardize a protocol → reorder every day

### Read
- Customer-success role description (shows how Stryker frames the job): https://careers.stryker.com/customer-success-associate-cleveland-injury-infection-prevention/job/4E2B8BB722702EAF89C9B2D53DDD1A45

- Sage news (browse 2024–2026 posts under https://www.stryker.com/us/en/sage/news/)

### Capstone projects (pick 2)
1. **One-page portfolio map:** every product family → HAC → key product → headline claim → source URL.
2. **Mock value-analysis pitch:** a 5-minute pitch to a hospital's value analysis committee for standardizing Sage oral care for NV-HAP. Include the problem, the protocol, the evidence (with caveats) and the cost-avoidance logic.
3. **Objection handling:** write answers to "We already use CHG wipes from another vendor," "Our nurses don't have time for oral care every 4 hours," and "External catheters leak."
4. **Teach-back:** explain Sage to a friend in 10 minutes with no notes.

### Final self-test (answer without looking)
1. When did Stryker close the Sage deal, for how much, and from whom?
2. Name the 4–5 Sage product families and the HAC each one targets.
3. What is Corinz, and which systems use it?
4. CHG cloth: dose per cloth, duration on the skin, and when it's applied pre-op.
5. Heel Protector II vs III: what did III add?
6. AirTAP vs TAP vs MATS: when do you use each?
7. PrimoFit vs PrimaFit vs PrimoFit Flow.
8. Who are the three types of stakeholders in a Sage sale?

## Quick-reference glossary
- **HAC**: hospital-acquired condition
- **CHG**: chlorhexidine gluconate (antiseptic)
- **BZK / BAC**: benzalkonium chloride
- **IFU**: instructions for use (the authoritative product document)
- **GPO**: group purchasing organization
- **VAC**: value analysis committee
- **SPHM**: safe patient handling & mobility
- **SCD**: sequential compression device
- **WOC nurse**: wound, ostomy & continence nurse
